## 1. Original mantra (मूल मन्त्र)

> **विश्वानि देव सवितर्दुरितानि परा सुव।**
> **यद्भद्रं तन्न आ सुव॥**
>
> *viśvāni deva savitar duritāni parā suva |*
> *yad bhadraṃ tan na ā suva ||*

| Parameter | Yajurveda 30.3 | Ṛgveda 5.82.5 |
|---|---|---|
| ऋषि (seer) | नारायण | श्यावाश्व आत्रेय |
| देवता (subject) | सविता | सविता |
| छन्दस् (metre) | गायत्री | गायत्री |
| स्वर (musical note of the metre) | षड्ज | षड्ज |

**Metre check (8 + 8 + 8 = 24 syllables, a pure gāyatrī):**

| Pāda | Syllables | Count |
|---|---|---|
| a | vi‑śvā‑ni / de‑va / sa‑vi‑tar | 8 |
| b | du‑ri‑tā‑ni / pa‑rā / su‑va | 8 |
| c | yad / bhad‑raṃ / tan / na / ā / su‑va | 8 |

**How the author reads the ṛṣi, devatā and chandas.** In the *Vedic raśmi theory* of the notes, the ṛṣi is the *generating ray* of a mantra:

- **Nārāyaṇa** = *nara* (subtle particles) + *ayana* (path). These are the *sūtrātmā-vāyu* rays that serve as pathways for prāṇa and other rays.
- **Śyāvāśva Ātreya** = rays that grow in increasing order from *Atri* (the sūtrātmā-vāyu rays).
- The devatā **Savitā** and the chandas **Gāyatrī** together make the affected substance "*śveta-varṇīya teja se yukta*" (endowed with white-coloured radiance).

The author's analogy: each chandas-ray carries the imprint of its ṛṣi-ray *"जिस प्रकार पुत्र वा पुत्री पर उसके माता-पिता आदि का प्रभाव होता है"* (as children carry the influence of their parents). Read in modern terms, the ṛṣi is the *source term*, the devatā the *field or agent*, and the chandas the *waveform or metric envelope*.

## 2. Methodological key: Nirukta 7.1–7.2 (S1)

> तास्त्रिविधा ऋचः। परोक्षकृताः प्रत्यक्षकृता आध्यात्मिक्यश्च। तत्र परोक्षकृताः सर्वाभिर्नामविभक्तिभिर्युज्यन्ते प्रथमपुरुषैश्चाख्यातस्य॥ अथ प्रत्यक्षकृता मध्यमपुरुषयोगाः। त्वमिति चैतेन सर्वनाम्ना। अथाध्यात्मिक्य उत्तमपुरुषयोगाः। अहमिति चैतेन सर्वनाम्ना॥

| Term | Grammar | Yāska's criterion | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| परोक्षकृता | परोक्ष + कृ + क्त (+टाप्), स्त्री. प्रथमा बहु. | all nominal cases + **3rd-person** verbs | The deity is described as "he/it" |
| प्रत्यक्षकृता | प्रत्यक्ष + कृ + क्त | **2nd-person** verb + *tvam* | The deity is addressed as "you" |
| आध्यात्मिकी | अध्यात्म + ठञ् (+ङीप्) | **1st-person** verb + *aham* | The speaker is the deity ("I") |

**What this shows.** Yāska is classifying mantras by **grammatical person, the mode of address** (प्रथम / मध्यम / उत्तम पुरुष). That is a different axis from the threefold **domain** of interpretation (adhidaivata / adhibhūta / adhyātma). The critics in the notes conflate the two. The author's point that they *"निरुक्त के इस प्रकरण का आशय नहीं समझ पाये"* (did not understand the purpose of this section of the Nirukta) is grammatically sound.

**Applied to M1:**

- The vocatives **देव, सवितः** and the 2nd-person imperative **सुव** make M1 **प्रत्यक्षकृता**.
- The enclitic **नः** ("for us") brings in the 1st-person standpoint of the speaker.

So one grammatical address frame carries three domains of interpretation. The *person* of a mantra is fixed by grammar. Its *domain* of reference is not.

## 3. Padaccheda (पदच्छेद) and sandhi-vigraha

> **विश्वानि । देव । सवितः । दुरितानि । परा । सुव । यत् । भद्रम् । तत् । नः । आ । सुव ॥**

| Surface form | Split | Sandhi rule (Aṣṭādhyāyī) |
|---|---|---|
| सवितर्दुरितानि | सवितर् + दुरितानि | Vocative stem-final र् stays before a voiced consonant. It does not become visarga, because खरवसानयोर्विसर्जनीयः (8.3.15) applies only before *khar* or a pause |
| यद्भद्रम् | यत् + भद्रम् | झलां जशोऽन्ते (8.2.39): त् → द् |
| भद्रं तन्न | भद्रम् + तत् | मोऽनुस्वारः (8.3.23): म् → ं before a consonant |
| तन्न | तत् + नः | यरोऽनुनासिकेऽनुनासिको वा (8.4.45): त् → न् before a nasal |
| न आ | नः + आ | Visarga is dropped before a vowel (भोभगोअघोअपूर्वस्य योऽशि 8.3.17 → लोपः शाकल्यस्य 8.3.19). The hiatus stays (no further sandhi) |

### 4.2 Grammatical observations

1. **One root, two opposite vectors.** The same verb √सू (*suva*) is used twice, with opposite preverbs: **परा** (away from) and **आ** (towards). The mantra therefore describes a *single generative impulse (प्रेरणा) with two opposite directions*: expulsion and attraction. This symmetry is the formal key to all 17 bhāṣyas.
2. **Number asymmetry.** दुरितानि is **plural** (many kinds of crookedness). भद्रम् is **singular** (one auspicious good). Disorder is many-valued; well-being converges on one thing, an attractor.
3. **Accent (स्वर).** देव and सवितः are vocatives that do not begin the pāda, so they are entirely unaccented (आमन्त्रितस्य च 8.1.19). सुव is a finite verb in a main clause, so it too is unaccented (तिङ्ङतिङः 8.1.28). This puts the prosodic weight on the content words: विश्वानि, दुरितानि, परा, यद्, भद्रम्, आ.
4. **Tmesis (separation of preverb and verb).** परा and आ stand apart from सुव, which is allowed only in the Veda (छन्दसि परेऽपि). The direction can therefore be stressed independently of the action.
5. **Dual reading of लोट्.** The author notes that *परा सुव* and *आ सुव* can be read in the लोट् lakāra as **prayer** (prārthanā) or as **expectation or duty** (apekṣā). Pāṇini supports both: लोट् च (3.3.162) covers विधि, निमन्त्रण, आमन्त्रण, अधीष्ट, सम्प्रश्न and प्रार्थना. This is why the ādhibhautika bhāṣyas read as *duties* of king, teacher, parent, scientist and physician ("…dūr karane kā prayatna kare").
6. **Relative–correlative clause (यद् … तद्).** यत् is nominative in its own clause and तत् is accusative in the main clause: "whatever is auspicious, *that* bring to us". The correlative structure works as an **open set definition**. It does not list what is bhadra; it defines bhadra functionally, so each domain fills in its own members.

## 5. Nirukta (निरुक्त): multi-dhātu derivations

Yāska's principle (Nirukta 2.1): **अर्थनित्यः परीक्षेत केनचिद्वृत्तिसामान्येन**, "always examine by meaning, through some shared function". Where one derivation does not fit, the Nirukta allows several (…इति वा …इति वा).

| पद | Nirukta derivations (several dhātus) | Layered senses |
|---|---|---|
| **विश्व** | (1) √विश् प्रवेशने: "that which enters everything" or "that into which everything enters". (2) √विश् as settling: "the settled totality" | All; pervasive; the totality of manifest entities; the universe |
| **देव** | Nirukta 7.15: **देवो दानाद्वा दीपनाद्वा द्योतनाद्वा द्युस्थानो भवतीति वा**. Also √दिव् with its ten senses (play, conquest, transaction, light, praise, joy, intoxication, sleep, desire, motion) | Giver (energy donor); illuminator; radiator; one located in dyu (the luminous region); a player or mover |
| **सवितृ** | (1) √षू प्रेरणे: impeller. (2) √षूङ् प्राणिप्रसवे: generator or begetter. Nirukta 10.31: **सविता सर्वस्य प्रसविता**. Brāhmaṇa: सविता वै देवानां प्रसविता (ŚB 1.1.2.17) | Impeller, generator, catalyst, source term; the one who produces and drives |
| **दुरित** | (1) दुर् + √इ (go): "bad going", wrong trajectory. (2) "that which is hard to traverse" (cf. दुर्ग). (3) दुर् + इत "fallen into the bad" | Deviated motion; crooked or unstable dynamics; obstacle; error; sin; disease; toxin; noise |
| **परा** | the preverb of "beyond, away, reversal" | Expulsion; repulsion; filtering out |
| **सुव** | √षू: impel. √सु (षुञ् अभिषवे): press, extract. √षूङ्: generate | Drive, send, generate, extract |
| **यद्/तद्** | pronominal | Open variable and its binding (a set-builder) |
| **भद्र** | Nirukta 4.10 (S2): **भजनीयं भूतानाम्** (√भज्, to be shared or resorted to) · **अभिद्रवणीयम्** (√द्रु, that towards which beings run) · **भवद् रमयति** (√भू + √रम्, that which, coming into being, delights) · **भाजनवत्** (√भज्, like a vessel or container). **The author's own use of the *bhadram* derivation (Bhāṣya 4):** *"जो कण आदि पदार्थ … उत्पन्न होते हुए केन्द्रीय भाग की ओर क्रीडा करते हुए दौड़ रहे होते हैं, उन्हें भद्र कहते हैं"* (particles that, as they arise, run playfully towards the central region are called *bhadra*). He takes Yāska's **अभिद्रवणीय** ("that which is run towards") literally, as *infall*. The Nirukta derivation becomes a physical description of accretion.

## 7. Contextual meaning (वाक्यार्थ)

**Anvaya (prose order):** हे देव सवितः! (त्वम्) विश्वानि दुरितानि परा सुव। यत् भद्रम् (अस्ति), तत् नः आ सुव।

**Literal:** "O radiant Impeller! Drive away all crooked ways. Whatever is auspicious, bring that to us from every side."

**Structural:** "O generative source! Using the same impulse, expel everything that destabilises and attract everything that sustains. Do this for the system we are."

**Dayānanda's Īśvara reading** (quoted by the author as the 17th bhāṣya): *"हे उत्तम गुण-कर्म-स्वभावयुक्त, उत्तम गुण-कर्म-स्वभावों में प्रेरणा देने वाले परमेश्वर! आप हमारे सब दुष्ट आचरण वा दुःखों को दूर कीजिये और जो कल्याणकारी धर्मयुक्त आचरण वा सुख है, उसको हमारे लिये अच्छे प्रकार उत्पन्न कीजिये।"* (O Lord, endowed with the best qualities, actions and nature, and inspiring them in us: remove all our wicked conduct and sorrows, and bring forth for us whatever conduct and happiness is beneficial and righteous.) The figure of speech is **वाचकलुप्तोपमालङ्कार**, a simile with the comparison word left out: as the worshipped Lord turns devotees from bad conduct, so a king should turn subjects from adharma to dharma, and do so himself.

## 8. The author's 17 bhāṣyas as one matrix

The fixed **operator structure** of the mantra is: *Savitā × (परा‑सुव ⟶ दुरित) × (आ‑सुव ⟶ भद्र) × नः*. Changing the value of the variable **Savitā** produces a different, internally consistent bhāṣya. The table below lists every bhāṣya in the notes.

| # | Category | सविता = | Evidence | दुरितानि (expelled) | भद्रम् (attracted) | नः |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | आधिदैविक | Sun (solar system) | असावादित्यो देवः सविता (ŚB 6.3.1.18) | Cosmic-cloud material moving in unstable or crooked orbits under *asura-vāyu* | Matter fit to join planets or the Sun | Sūtrātmā-vāyu rays and the lokas they bind |
| 2 | आधिदैविक | Heat (ūṣmā) | उष्णमेव सविता (GB Pūrva 1.33) | Low-energy particles whose motion is disordered and cannot bond | The right energy level for each process | Particles and substances |
| 3 | आधिदैविक | Electricity (vidyut) | विद्युदेव सविता (GB Pūrva 1.33) | Crooked or undesirable motions and their causes | Straight (ṛju) and desired motions; correct direction at bonding | Particles via sūtrātmā rays |
| 4 | आधिदैविक | Solar core | सविता राष्ट्रम् (ŚB 11.4.3.3); सविता वै देवानां प्रसविता (ŚB 1.1.2.17) | Particles lacking enough energy for fusion, which are sent back | Particles "running playfully towards the centre" (अभिद्रवणीय) | Particles of the solar body |

| 17 | आध्यात्मिक | Parameśvara (Dayānanda) | Yajurveda-bhāṣya 30.3 | Wicked conduct and sorrows | Righteous conduct and happiness | Us, the people |

**Observation.** The 17 rows behave like **17 models of a single formal schema**. In mathematical logic one set of axioms can have many models; in linguistics one syntactic frame can host many lexical values. The author's argument, *"यह बड़ी अविद्या की बात है"* that a mantra has only one meaning, is in effect an argument that the mantra is a **schema with free variables**, not a single closed proposition.

## 9. Philosophical interpretation

### 9.1 Vedānta

| पद | Vedāntic reading |
|---|---|
| देव सवितः | Brahman as the impeller of all functions: *"यः … अन्तरो यमयति"* (Bṛhad. Up. 3.7, the antaryāmin) |
| विश्वानि दुरितानि | Avidyā and its effects: the adhyāsa (superimposition) of non-self on self, and the bondage of karma |
| परा सुव | Sublation (bādha) of avidyā by vidyā: *"ज्ञानाग्निः सर्वकर्माणि भस्मसात्कुरुते"* (Gītā 4.37) |
| यद्भद्रम् | Ānanda, the bliss-nature of Brahman: *"आनन्दो ब्रह्मेति व्यजानात्"* (Taitt. Up. 3.6) |
| आ सुव | Not a bringing-in from outside but the *uncovering* of what is already present: *āvaraṇa-bhaṅga* |
| नः | The jīvas, who in Advaita are not ultimately different from the Savitṛ invoked |

**Insight.** In Advaita the two motions turn out to be one. Removing the *duritāni* (avidyā) *is* the arrival of *bhadra* (Brahman-bliss), because bliss was never absent. This fits the author's Bhāṣya 1 mechanics, where one force (attraction) does both jobs: it nullifies the asura effect and draws in the bhadra particles.

### 9.2 Sāṅkhya

| पद | Sāṅkhya reading |
|---|---|
| सविता | The *proximity-impulse* of puruṣa that sets prakṛti moving (Sāṅkhya-kārikā 21, *paṅgv-andha-vat*); in theistic Sāṅkhya, Īśvara as nimitta |
| विश्वानि | Every evolute of prakṛti (the 23 tattvas) |
| दुरितानि | Excess **tamas** (inertia, obscuration) and disordered **rajas** (turbulence) |
| भद्रम् | **Sattva**: lightness (laghu), illumination (prakāśaka). Sāṅkhya-kārikā 13: *सत्त्वं लघु प्रकाशकमिष्टम्* |
| परा / आ | Shifting the guṇa balance (guṇa-vaiṣamya) away from tamas and towards sattva |
| नः | Buddhi and ahaṅkāra, the faculties in which the guṇa balance becomes experience |

**Insight.** In Sāṅkhya terms the mantra is a request for **guṇa re-equilibration**. The author's adhidaivika bhāṣyas (low-energy disordered particles as *durita*, ordered bonding as *bhadra*) closely match Sāṅkhya's *tamas* (inert, obstructive) and *sattva* (ordered, luminous). The *Gāyatrī* metre, whose rays the notes call *śveta-varṇa teja*, is traditionally associated with sattva.

### 9.3 Yoga

| पद | Yoga reading (Yoga-sūtra) |
|---|---|
| सविता | Īśvara (YS 1.24: *क्लेशकर्मविपाकाशयैरपरामृष्टः पुरुषविशेष ईश्वरः*), or the self-luminous seer (draṣṭā) |
| दुरितानि | The five **kleśas** (YS 2.3) and their stored traces (āśaya) |
| परा सुव | **Pratiprasava** (YS 2.10: *ते प्रतिप्रसवहेयाः सूक्ष्माः*). The same root √सू appears negated: dissolving back to the source. Also the author's *dagdha-bīja* ("burnt seed") |
| यद्भद्रम् | **Viveka-khyāti** (discriminative insight, YS 2.26) and prajñā *ṛtambharā* (YS 1.48) |
| आ सुव | Cultivation through abhyāsa (practice) and vairāgya (detachment) (YS 1.12) |
| नः | Citta and its vṛttis |

**Insight.** Patañjali's term *prati-pra-sava* (YS 2.10) is built on the **same root √सू** as *Savitṛ* and *suva*. Yoga's way of removing kleśas is literally a "*parā-suva*": reversing the generative impulse back to its source. This lexical link between the mantra and the Yoga-sūtra is grammatically exact.

## 10. Scientific interpretation (word → concept mapping)

> **Methodological note.** The mappings below are **structural correspondences** (homologies of function). They are not claims that the mantra's author held modern theories. Their value is heuristic: they show that the mantra's operator structure (one source, two opposite vectors, an open set of goods) recurs in many natural systems. Where the uploaded notes make empirical claims that go beyond current physics, this is flagged in §10.6.

### 10.1 Physics (energy, fields, waves, thermodynamics)

| पद | Physical analogue | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| सविता | **Source term** in a field equation (∇·E = ρ/ε₀; the stress-energy tensor as the source of curvature) | √सू (to generate or impel) corresponds to whatever drives a field |
| देव | **Radiative emitter** | "dīpanāt dyotanāt": luminosity |
| विश्वानि | **All modes** of the system (the full spectrum, every degree of freedom) | |
| दुरितानि | **Unstable or chaotic trajectories, decoherent or dissipative modes, noise** | "duḥ-ita" = bad path; compare positive Lyapunov exponents |
| परा सुव | **Repulsive potential, radiation pressure, damping** | "away" vector |
| भद्रम् | **Bound and stable states, attractors, coherent modes** | Yāska's *abhidravaṇīya* = "that towards which things run" = the basin of an attractor |
| आ सुव | **Attractive potential, accretion, resonant absorption** | "hither" vector |
| नः | **The system boundary or control volume** | |

**Thermodynamic reading: Savitā as Maxwell's demon with an energy budget.** "Expel the disordered, admit the ordered" is a sorting operation. Sorting lowers local entropy. By Landauer's principle and the second law, it must be paid for with energy and must export entropy. The Sun pays this cost for the Earth: it delivers *low-entropy* photons (~5,800 K) and the Earth radiates *high-entropy* infrared (~255 K). That gradient powers every ordering process in the biosphere. In thermodynamic language the mantra asks the **low-entropy source** to do the sorting work that local order depends on. This is why the "Sun" bhāṣya (1), the "heat" bhāṣya (2) and the "earth" bhāṣya (6) belong together.

### 10.2 Cosmology and astrophysics

| Bhāṣya | Notes' description | Modern correspondence |
|---|---|---|
| 8 (cosmic cloud) | Cloud compresses and fragments to form lokas; "Indra's vajra" at the outer layer repels asura matter | **Molecular-cloud collapse** (Jeans instability) and fragmentation; **shock fronts and magnetic pressure** at cloud boundaries |
| 8 (asura needed) | A measured amount of asura substance is needed to keep planets away from the Sun | Formally like **angular momentum / centrifugal support**: without a counter-tendency to gravity, all matter would fall into the centre |
| 1 (Sun) | Strong solar attraction overcomes asura-vāyu; light rays "strike" asura matter | **Gravitational binding plus radiation pressure and solar wind** clearing the inner disk (the protoplanetary disk dissipates within ~10 million years) |
| 4 (solar core) | Particles without enough energy for bonding are sent back; *bhadra* particles are drawn in | **Coulomb barrier and Gamow peak**: only protons in the high-energy tail, helped by quantum tunnelling, fuse. The rest scatter |
| 9 (particles and photons) | Constant exchange of rays; the overall quantity stays nearly conserved | **Virtual-particle exchange** (field quanta mediate forces) and conservation laws |

### 10.3 Neuroscience

| पद | Neural analogue |
|---|---|
| सविता | **Ascending neuromodulatory systems** (locus coeruleus, VTA dopamine, basal-forebrain acetylcholine) that "impel" (√षू प्रेरणे) cortical states |
| विश्वानि दुरितानि | Maladaptive patterns: intrusive thoughts, noise, prediction errors, pathological oscillations |
| परा सुव | **Inhibitory gating** (thalamic reticular nucleus, GABAergic interneurons); **synaptic pruning** in development; glymphatic waste clearance during sleep |
| यद्भद्रम् | **Salient, reward-predictive, adaptive** signals |
| आ सुव | **Long-term potentiation** (Hebbian strengthening); attentional amplification |
| नः | The organism's self-model (interoceptive / default-mode network) |

**Insight.** The mantra's two-sided structure matches the brain's **excitation–inhibition balance**. Healthy cognition needs both active suppression (*parā*) and selective amplification (*ā*) from the same modulatory source. When the E/I balance fails, for example in epilepsy, the "duritāni" are no longer driven away.

### 10.4 Systems theory

| पद | Systems-theoretic role |
|---|---|
| सविता | **Controller or regulator**, the driving input |
| दुरितानि | **Disturbances and noise** (d(t)) |
| परा सुव | **Negative feedback and filtering**: rejecting disturbances |
| भद्रम् | **Reference signal or set-point** |
| आ सुव | **Feed-forward supply and positive selection** |
| नः | **The plant** (the system being controlled) |

**Insight.** By Ashby's **law of requisite variety**, a regulator must have as many kinds of response as there are kinds of disturbance. The mantra pairs **विश्वानि** (all) with the **plural** दुरितानि, so the controller must answer every type of disturbance. The singular **भद्रम्** is the single set-point. Bhāṣya 16 (the liver) is a textbook homeostatic regulator: detoxification is the *parā* branch and synthesis (bile, albumin, clotting factors) is the *ā* branch.

### 10.5 Social science (the ādhibhautika series)

Bhāṣyas 10–14 (king, teacher, parents, scientist, physician) are five **institutional regulators**, each of which reduces a particular kind of social entropy:

| Institution | Durita reduced | Bhadra supplied | Modern analogue |
|---|---|---|---|
| King (state) | Violence, crime, disaster loss | Public goods, justice | Governance and rule of law |
| Teacher | Ignorance | Knowledge | Education systems |
| Parents | Maladaptive habits | Character, health | Early-childhood development |
| Scientist | Pollution, harmful technology | Sustainable technology | Environmental science and engineering ethics |
| Physician | Pathogens, toxins | Health and strength | Public health and preventive medicine |

The author's remark, *"किसी भी राष्ट्र को सुखी बनाने का दायित्व राजा से भी अधिक वैज्ञानिक का होता है"* (the scientist, more than the king, is responsible for a nation's happiness), sees technology as the strongest lever on a society's environmental *durita*.

### 10.6 Critical calibration of the notes' scientific claims

A research-level reading should separate three kinds of statement:

| Claim in the notes | Status |
|---|---|
| Heat is present in all processes; "zero" on a temperature scale does not mean zero energy | **Consistent** with physics: zero-point energy exists even at absolute zero |
| A solar core that "sends back" particles without enough energy | **A good analogy** for the Coulomb barrier and Gamow peak |
| The liver separates dead red blood cells and detoxifies | **Broadly correct**. The spleen does most red-cell removal; the liver's Kupffer cells take part and process the bilirubin |
| "Vacuum can be called vāyu-tattva"; vāyu makes up for apparent breaks in conservation "which modern science does not know" | **An interpretive proposal, not established physics.** Modern physics has observed no breach of energy–momentum conservation. Vacuum fluctuations are already described within QFT, so this is better treated as a *mapping* of vāyu-tattva onto the quantum vacuum |
| Remarks on modern medicine as a whole | **The author's opinion**; not evaluated here, and not needed for the mantra analysis |

## 12. Interdisciplinary insight: śabda – artha – tattva

### 12.1 The triad

| Level | In M1 | Theory |
|---|---|---|
| **Śabda** (word, form) | 12 padas, gāyatrī metre, two identical verbs with opposite preverbs, plural/singular contrast, yad–tad correlative | Pāṇinian grammar fixes *what may be said* |
| **Artha** (meaning) | A **schema**: Generator → expel disorder, attract order → for the system | Yāska fixes *which senses each word may carry* (multiple dhātus; *vṛtti-sāmānya*) |
| **Tattva** (reality) | Every self-maintaining system: star, cloud, cell, liver, brain, school, state, self | Each tattva is a *model* satisfying the schema |

### 12.2 Why "ananta artha" is not arbitrary

The author is careful to say: *"हमने यहाँ अपनी कल्पना से भाष्य नहीं किए हैं… बिना सुतर्क और ऊहा के कोई भी व्यक्ति शास्त्रों एवं आधुनिक विज्ञान दोनों के ही मर्म को नहीं समझ सकता"* (we have not made these bhāṣyas up; without sound reasoning and inference no one can grasp the essence of either the śāstras or modern science). Yāska gives the warrant and the limit together (Nirukta 13.12): **"अयं मन्त्रार्थचिन्ताभ्यूहोऽभ्यूढः। अपि श्रुतितोऽपि तर्कतः। न तु पृथक्त्वेन मन्त्रा निर्वक्तव्याः, प्रकरणश एव तु निर्वक्तव्याः।"** Reflection on mantra meaning works by inference (ūha), drawn from both scripture and reasoning, but mantras are to be explained in context, not word by word in isolation. He also recounts that when the ṛṣis departed, the gods gave humanity **Tarka (reasoning) as their ṛṣi**.

From the notes, four **validity constraints** follow for any new bhāṣya:

1. **Grammatical admissibility**: every word keeps its case, number, person and lakāra.
2. **Nirukta warrant**: every sense is reachable from an attested dhātu or brāhmaṇa equation (the 14 *Savitā* statements are such warrants).
3. **Intra-bhāṣya consistency**: once Savitā = X, the words durita, bhadra and naḥ must all take values from X's domain.
4. **Functional fit (vṛtti-sāmānya)**: the X chosen must actually show the expel/attract structure.

The author's 17 bhāṣyas meet all four, which is what separates *ananta artha* from free association.

### 12.3 Summary formula

> **Śabda** (a fixed syntactic operator) **× Nirukta** (a lawful lexical range) **→ Artha-schema** (one source, two vectors, an open set of goods) **→ Tattva-models** (from cosmos to cell to society to self)

A Vedic mantra, on this analysis, works as a **compressed generative grammar of reality**: a template whose instances run across scales. That is the precise sense in which it is a *multi-layer encoded knowledge system*.
